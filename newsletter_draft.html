Finnish Researchers Win Award for Privacy-Preserving AI Training Techniques

Researchers from the University of Helsinki and the Finnish Center for Artificial Intelligence (FCAI), in collaboration with NVIDIA, have developed a method that ensures privacy in AI training while making the process faster and more memory-efficient. Their approach, which won the Best Paper award at the IEEE Conference on Secure and Trustworthy Machine Learning in March 2026, leverages differential privacy to protect sensitive data, such as medical information, during training. The technique was tested on computer vision models using Finland’s CSC supercomputers. This development is crucial for educational institutions and organizations handling sensitive data, as it supports ethical AI practices and enhances data security in digital learning environments.

Interactive Visualizer Enhances AI Training Through User Feedback

Researchers from Aalto University, the University of Trento, and KTH Royal Institute of Technology developed an interactive visualizer to enhance AI training by allowing users to explore and compare simulated robotic movements. The tool enables users to provide more informative feedback, leading to AI models that performed up to 60% better in tests. While users found the visualizer more efficient, they noted it was less intuitive than traditional comparison methods. The study highlights the potential of human-AI collaboration in improving machine learning processes, offering insights for educators and EdTech developers seeking to integrate user feedback into AI systems.

FCAI and ELLIS Institute Finland Launch New AI Research Initiatives

In February, Finland’s FCAI joined Nordic AI centers in forming the Nordic AI Partnership, aiming to boost collaboration in research, innovation, and education. The AI Doctoral Program, involving 10 universities and 100 students, received an Impact Award for its innovative PhD training model. ELLIS Institute Finland officially launched in November, becoming the newest expansion of the European Laboratory for Learning and Intelligent Systems. The institute focuses on advancing AI and machine learning research, with a call for principal investigators open until January 12, 2026. These developments offer valuable insights for professionals in AI education and research.

Finnish AI Research Thrives on Collaboration and Shared Resources

A Finnish researcher discusses how collaboration within Finland's strong AI research network, particularly through the Finnish Center for Artificial Intelligence (FCAI) and the ELLIS Institute, is driving innovation in machine learning. By using approximate inference methods, the researcher and colleagues from Aalto University and University College London are addressing computational challenges in AI model training. The article emphasizes Finland's ability to attract talent and foster a seamless collaborative culture, which is seen as a key factor in producing high-quality, impactful research. This environment supports both academic and practical advancements in AI, making it highly relevant for professionals in digital learning and EdTech.

13 AI Programs in Finland Offer English-Taught Master's Degrees for 2026

As of October 2026, Finland offers 13 artificial intelligence master's programs at 10 universities, all taught in English. International students can pursue these programs with annual fees averaging $13,448. The uniformity in language and pricing makes these programs attractive for global learners. For education professionals, this highlights Finland's growing prominence in AI education and its commitment to international accessibility.
